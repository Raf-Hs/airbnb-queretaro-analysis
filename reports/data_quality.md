than the whole sample. This is stated alongside the capacity finding.

## 4. The `blocked_days` trap

This is the single most important schema quirk in the dataset.

```
reserved_days + available_days = total_days      → 100/100 listings, exactly 365
blocked_days ⊆ available_days                    → verified
```

`blocked_days` is **not** a third calendar category. If you model
`reserved + available + blocked`, you double-count supply and every occupancy
figure becomes wrong.

Consequences:

- **Calendar occupancy** (headline) = `reserved / total`. Blocked nights count as
  not-earned. Conservative and comparable across listings.
- **Adjusted occupancy** = `reserved / (total − blocked)`. Answers a different
  question: "when open, did it sell?"
- 48 of 100 listings blocked at least one night; among those the median is 63.5
  blocked nights, and the maximum is 330 of 365 (a listing that took only 30
  booked nights and blocked the rest — high adjusted occupancy, near-zero
  calendar occupancy).
- City sample: mean occupancy 26.5% → 31.9% under the adjusted definition;
  medians 24.1% → 27.8%.

Both definitions are reported. Only calendar occupancy is used for headline
claims.

## 5. Right-censoring (the issue that would otherwise fake a crash)

July 2026 is the snapshot month. Bookings not yet made and reviews not yet
written make the tail of the window incomplete.

Evidence — monthly review volume, which lags stays:

| Month | Reviews | Booked listings | Calendar occupancy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Aug 2025 | 101 | 75 | 33.6% |
| Sep 2025 | 98 | 72 | 29.9% |
| Oct 2025 | 124 | 75 | 32.3% |
| Nov 2025 | 132 | 77 | 31.0% |
| Dec 2025 | 132 | 79 | 33.9% |
| Jan 2026 | 104 | 68 | 27.0% |
| Feb 2026 | 101 | 72 | 31.9% |
| Mar 2026 | 88 | 73 | 32.8% |
| Apr 2026 | 104 | 70 | 25.1% |
| May 2026 | 73 | 58 | 19.8% |
| Jun 2026 | 25 | 55 | 14.1% |
| Jul 2026 | 6 | 44 | 8.3% |

The break between April and May is sharp and monotonic (101 → 73 → 25 → 6
reviews). This is an observation artefact, not a demand collapse.

**Decision:** all realised-demand claims use **Aug 2025 – Apr 2026**. May–Jul
2026 are plotted with a shaded censored region and excluded from every
occupancy/revenue conclusion. Asking prices for those months are still
observable (hosts set them in advance) and are used only with an explicit
"asking price" label.

## 6. Zero-booking months

| Scope | Zero-booking listing-months | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| All listings | 369 / 1,195 | 30.9% |
| City sample | 365 / 1,183 | 30.9% |
| Listings with ≥ 1 such month | 79 / 100 | — |

These are real: 1,195 rows include months where a listing received no bookings.

Handling:

- `adr_booked_mxn` is null exactly when `reserved_days = 0` (369/369 match), so
  realised ADR is never fabricated.
- `adr_quoted_mxn` remains populated, so quoted-price trends still use every
  listing-month.
- Monthly occupancy handles zeros correctly because it is computed from
  `reserved / (reserved + vacant)`, not by averaging non-zero months.
- Revenue sums include zeros naturally.

**Do not** compute "average ADR" over `adr_booked_mxn` without acknowledging that
the denominator excludes unsold listing-months. That would overstate realised
prices.

## 7. Partial history: listing `13845232`

One listing has **7 of 12 months**. Partial coverage is visible directly in
`monthly_rates` (7 rows for this ID), and totals reconcile across the dataset
(9,637 reserved nights across all 100 listings).

- Flagged `is_partial_history = true` at the row level.
- Its TTM fields use the provider's fixed 365-day denominator: 57 reserved nights
  ÷ 365 = 15.6% occupancy. Its monthly rows cover only 212 days, where the same
  57 nights imply 26.9%. This project does not rescale the TTM figure; the
  listing is retained with the discrepancy documented.
- Excluded from the paired within-listing price comparison (98 listings used,
  not 99).

Impact: this listing contributes 0.5% of sample revenue (MX$60,488). Retaining it
with a flag is more honest than deleting it or rescaling it.

## 8. Revenue vs ADR × nights: the fee gap

| Check | Value |
|---|---|
| TTM revenue (listings) | MX$11,995,861 |
| Sum of monthly revenue | MX$11,505,194 |
| Difference | MX$499,002 (**+4.3%**) |
| TTM reserved nights | 9,588 |
| Sum of monthly reserved nights | 9,588 (**exact**) |

Nights reconcile perfectly; money does not. The gap is consistent with fees
(cleaning, extra-guest) that the TTM revenue field includes but the monthly
nightly-rate field does not.

**Decision:** `revenue_mxn` is called *listing revenue*, never *gross booking
value* or *host payout*. No attempt was made to redistribute the MX$499k across
months, because that would fabricate an allocation the data does not support.

## 10. Outliers: flagged, not deleted

`is_high_price_outlier` uses `Q3 + 3 × IQR` on `price_mxn`, flagging 6 listings
(MX$5,184–11,176/night). They are genuinely large homes (3–9 bedrooms), one a
rural estate, not data-entry errors: capacity, bathroom count, and revenue all
scale with them.

| Scope | n | Mean ADR | Mean occupancy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| City sample | 99 | MX$1,303 | 26.5% |
| Excluding 6 high-price flags | 94 | MX$1,016 | 27.1% |
| All 100 (incl. rural) | 100 | MX$1,402 | 26.4% |

Means move by 22–29%; medians barely move. This is why the analysis reports
medians with bootstrap CIs and treats means as context.

## 11. Location precision

- 55 listings have `exact_location = false`; 6 have it null. Coordinates are
  approximate (roughly block/colonia level).
- 100/100 points fell inside a SEPOMEX polygon, so postal labels are reliable at
  the polygon level — but a postal code is not a neighbourhood.
- Distance to Plaza de Armas is straight-line, not travel distance.

Effect on findings: the near-zero distance–price correlation (ρ = −0.013) is
reported as "no detectable gradient", not "location is irrelevant". Approximation
error attenuates observed effects, so a true gradient could exist and be
invisible here.

## 13. What "24 checks passed" does not mean

The checks verify **internal consistency** — arithmetic identities, key
uniqueness, ranges, referential integrity. They do **not** verify:

1. **Representativeness.** Nothing in the file proves the 100 listings are a
   random sample of the ~1,733-listing market. The provider calls it
   representative; that claim is not independently testable here.
2. **Provider accuracy.** If AirROI mis-computes occupancy, this pipeline will
   reproduce the error consistently. Two independent occupancy identities do
   agree, which rules out arithmetic slips, not upstream definition choices.
3. **Booking completeness.** Reserved nights are only as complete as the
   provider's scrape history. There is no independent booking ledger to check
   them against.
4. **Coordinate truth.** Approximate coordinates cannot be validated.
5. **Stationarity.** A passing check says the snapshot is coherent, not that its
   relationships hold over time.

These limits are carried into `reports/findings.md` and the README rather than
being buried here.
